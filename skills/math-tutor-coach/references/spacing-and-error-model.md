# Basis for the spacing intervals and error taxonomy

[TRAINING] — cognitive-science literature, stable/non-high-drift.

- **Retrieval practice > re-study**: Roediger & Karpicke; testing effect is one of the more robustly replicated findings in learning science. This is why the maintenance deck poses problems cold before offering any hint.
- **Spacing effect**: Cepeda et al. meta-analysis; expanding intervals beat massed practice for retention. The 1/3/7/14-day ladder here is a compression of standard SRS scheduling (e.g., Anki's default-ish shape) to fit inside a 7-week term rather than a semester.
- **Interleaving effect**: Rohrer & Taylor — mixed-topic practice produces worse in-session performance but better retention/transfer than blocked practice. This is why maintenance sessions interleave courses/topics rather than reviewing one course's backlog at a time.
- **Long-term retention / overlearning**: Bahrick's foreign-language-vocabulary decay studies — content practiced only to bare initial mastery decays over years; content "overlearned" (continued past initial mastery, or built on by later coursework) is retained far longer. This is the direct justification for treating "completed course, not currently being built on" as the decay-risk category the maintenance deck targets.
- **Math anxiety and working memory**: Ashcraft & Kirk; Beilock — anxiety competes with working memory during problem-solving, independent of actual skill. Relevant to the tone guidance (don't console away a miss, but don't grade-pressure a maintenance session either — it's explicitly low-stakes by design).
- **Error taxonomy (a/b/c/d)**: not a single named framework from one source — it's a practical merge of prerequisite-gap diagnosis (common in mastery-learning approaches, e.g. Bloom) and standard slip-vs-misconception distinctions used in math-ed error analysis. Treat the taxonomy as a useful operational tool, not a citation-backed instrument.
