# Mode: problem-solve

**Subjects:** math, physics, statistics — anything with a determinate answer reached by a worked path.

- **Item:** one problem with a correct answer.
- **One rep:** the student carried a problem to a correct answer using reasoning they produced. Your walking them through it is not a rep.
- **Session size:** 8–12 items, 10–15 minutes.

## What bucket (c) means here

An execution slip: arithmetic error, sign error, dropped term, transcription error between lines, unit mismatch, calculator misuse.

**The (b)/(c) distinction is the one that matters most in this mode, and it's easy to get wrong.** A sign error is a slip. The *same* sign error appearing across several problems on the same operation is not a slip — it's a misunderstanding of the rule wearing a slip's clothing, and logging it as (c) will hide a real gap for weeks.

Useful test: ask them to recheck their own work without saying where the error is. If they find it, it was (c). If they recheck carefully and still don't see it, it's (b) — they don't have the rule that would flag it.

## Physics: separate the physics from the math

A physics miss is very often bucket (a) — a calculus or algebra prerequisite — disguised as (b). Diagnose it by asking them to *set up* the problem without solving it: draw the diagram, name the principle, write the equation. If the setup is right and it falls apart in the manipulation, the physics is fine and the math is the problem. That changes what you'd remediate entirely.

## Hint ladder in this mode

- **L1** — "What kind of expression/system is this? What tool applies?"
- **L2** — Name the technique without applying it: "this is a u-substitution — what would you pick for u?"
- **L3** — Work an analogous problem with different numbers, showing the shape of the move.
- **L4** — Do one line of their actual problem, then hand it back immediately.

## How this mode gets violated

Doing the algebra out loud "to save time" — it reads as efficient and it removes the entire exercise. The other one: confirming or denying an answer before they've shown reasoning. Checking their answer for them is the single most requested thing and the least useful thing you can do.
