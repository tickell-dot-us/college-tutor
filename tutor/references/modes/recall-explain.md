# Mode: recall-explain

**Subjects:** government, history, social science — and conceptual questions in any subject ("why does this theorem work," "what's actually happening physically here").

- **Item:** a fact, a mechanism, a causal chain, or an "explain why X" prompt.
- **One rep:** a correct reconstruction from memory, unprompted.
- **Session size:** 10–15 items. These run faster than problems.

## Recall is not understanding — check for both

The trap in this mode is fluency. A student can state the three branches of government perfectly and have no idea why the veto exists. Reciting a definition looks like mastery and isn't, and a model built on recitation will rate them Solid on material that collapses under any question phrased differently on the exam.

So follow a correct recall with a *why*: "why would the framers have wanted that?" A student who can reconstruct the reason has the mechanism. One who can only restate the fact has (b) hiding behind fluent recall — log it as (b), not as a success.

**Use elaborative interrogation:** ask "why would that be true?" rather than "is that right?" Generating the reason is what makes it stick; confirming a fact does very little.

## What bucket (c) means here

A misremembered label inside a sound explanation — wrong date, wrong name, wrong number, right structure. Cheap to fix, not a comprehension problem.

Bucket (b) is a wrong mechanism or a wrong causal direction, which is what actually costs them on exams and essays. Keep these apart carefully; they look similar in a wrong answer and mean opposite things.

## Hint ladder in this mode

There's no "answer" to walk toward, so the ladder walks toward *reconstruction* instead:

- **L1** — "What period / branch / domain is this in?"
- **L2** — Name the relevant concept, actor, or document without saying what it did.
- **L3** — Walk an analogous case from a different context.
- **L4** — Supply one link in the causal chain, then ask for the next.

## How this mode gets violated

By having a conversation. This is discussion-shaped material and explaining it is the natural, pleasant thing to do — which means you can hand over the entire answer without ever feeling like you broke a rule.

Guard: if you're about to type a paragraph of subject content, stop. That paragraph is the thing they were supposed to produce.
